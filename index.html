🌿 Lecture 01: Feed Resources & Forage Conservation
Core classification • TDN • roughages

📘 Nutrition & definitions

Nutrition: Interprets relationship between nutrients & organisms (ingestion → digestion → absorption → metabolism → excretion). Important for growth, production, reproduction.
Feed vs Food: Feed = single component (green forage). Food = mixture (green forage + wanda + straw).

🗺️ FEED RESOURCES MAP
├─ ROUGHAGES (crude fiber ↑, TDN ↓)
│ ├─ Green roughages: Legumes (Berseem, Lucern) / Non-legumes (Bajra, Maize, Sorghum, Oat)
│ └─ Dry roughages: Wheat straw, Rice polish, Hay
├─ CONCENTRATES (TDN ↑)
│ ├─ Energy rich: Cereal grains (plant), Fat (animal)
│ └─ Protein rich: Soybean meal, Cottonseed cake (plant); Fish meal, Blood meal (animal)
├─ AGRO-INDUSTRIAL BYPRODUCTS: Bagasse, Sugarcane toppings, Molasses, Sugarbeet pulp
└─ FEED ADDITIVES: Nutritional (vitamins), Non-nutritional (colors, binders), Antibiotics

High stress, disease, high herd density, vaccination period, anti-nutritional factors (aflatoxin), large particle size

💊 Lecture 03: Feed Additives
Nutritional • Non‑nutritional • Medical
Definition: Ingredients added to basic feed/ration to enhance nutritive value or other specific function.

🥗 Nutritional

Multivitamins, molasses, mineral mixture (DCP, LS)

🎨 Non‑nutritional

Physical (colors, mould inhibitors), Chemical (sodium bentonite), Biological (mint, garlic, ginger for acidosis, turmeric, lemons)

🏥 Medical

Antibiotics (Linco doxy), Probiotics (yogurt), Insulin, Progesterone, Oxytocin (milk letdown), Placenta removal, Estrogen
🧫 Biological additives: Mint → relieves bloat/tympany; Garlic/Ginger → acidosis; Fat cutters → reduce reproductive issues.

🐏 Lecture 04: Feeding Practices – Small Ruminants
Grazing • Stall • Combined

🌾 Continuous grazing

Same pasture all year, water given but no management → low gain/acre, waste, loss of desirable species.

🔄 Rotational grazing

Paddocks, rest period (e.g. 28d rest / 4d graze) → deeper roots, more production.

🐐 Mixed grazing

Goats + sheep + cattle together on same pasture.
🏠 Stall feeding (intensive): animals tied, no free choice – farmer provides balanced diet (hay, grain, additives) → ensures health & max production.
🏡 Combined (grazing+stall): villages, sheep are selective grazers, goats are not. Adult sheep eats ~7 kg green fodder daily.
🎯 Concentrates given 21 days before lambing and 45 days after lambing (healthy lamb crop).
Lambing = giving birth in sheep.
Feeding systemKey feature
Continuous grazingNo rotation, low productivity
Rotational grazingPaddocks, rest intervals
Stall feedingComplete diet provided by farmer
⚙️ Lecture 05: Methods of Improving Feed Quality
Physical • Chemical • Biological

🔧 Physical

Grinding, chopping, pelleting, soaking, steaming → ↑ digestibility, ↑ intake, less waste.

🧪 Chemical

Urea treatment: 2-4% urea + water, spray on wheat straw, store 15 days (anaerobic) → CP 3% → 8.5%. Also molasses-urea blocks.

🧫 Biological

Silage making, probiotics/enzymes, fungal treatments (degrade lignin).
⚠️ Anti-nutritional factors: Gossypol, Nitrates, Oxalates → removed by heat treatment / hot air torch (e.g., maize).
🧂 Feed additives for quality: buffers (prevent acidosis), antioxidants, toxin binders.

📦 Storage & handling

Dry, ventilated areas; avoid fungal contamination; airtight systems for silage/concentrates.
🌾 Lecture 06: Silage & Hay Making
Preservation methods

🥬 Silage

Moisture 70-75% • stored in silo • pressed layers + molasses/salts • covered with plastic (anaerobic) • fermentation by Clostridia → lactic acid → improves palatability • stored 2 months.

🌿 Hay

Moisture 20-25% • fully mature plants • stored in shade • turned daily • no fermentation.
🧫 Clostridia = anaerobic bacteria that produce lactic acid, making silage more palatable and preserving nutrients.

📐 Lecture 07: Feed Formulation
Pearson Square • 4 methods
Definition: Combining 2+ ingredients to make a fixed ration fulfilling nutritional needs.
4 METHODS
1️⃣ Trial & error (Antoine L.) – time‑consuming, expensive, animal harm → abandoned.
2️⃣ Pearson's square – only for 2 ingredients.
3️⃣ Algebraic – prone to human error.
4️⃣ Computerized – accurate but software expensive.

⭐ Pearson square (exam example) ⭐

Goal: 16% CP
Ingredient A: Wheat 48% CP
Ingredient B: Rice 11% CP

Wheat 48% → (16 - 11) = 5 parts Wheat
Rice 11% → (48 - 16) = 32 parts Rice
Total = 37 parts → Wheat % = 5/37 ≈ 13.5%   Rice % = 32/37 ≈ 86.5%

✅ Mix 13.5% wheat + 86.5% rice = 16% CP ration.
Memory: “Big minus middle goes to opposite corner”.

📉 Least‑cost formulation = cheapest balanced ration. Precision feeding reduces waste.
